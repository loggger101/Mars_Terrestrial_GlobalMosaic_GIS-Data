An audit of the 512 labels then revised the scheme to seven classes: Crater narrowed to impact craters, volcanic
plains drawn as Normal Ground, and three new classes, volcanic edifice, apron and fluvial channel. Proposals for each
label and new polygons for the new classes were built from the geologic map, the Robbins database, the IAU names, the
elevation model and the valley map of \citet{Alemanno2018}. Every relabel, check and edifice outline was reviewed by
eye; the apron and fluvial-strip proposals were screened by a rule written before it ran. The result, 971 labels, keeps
the same 14 held-out blocks (734 training and 237 held-out polygons), and a support vector machine and a U-Net were
retrained on it exactly as before. The seven-class map was to replace the published one only if, on the same v2
held-out polygons, (a) its accuracy and \kap\ on the ground both maps can name (v2 classes 1--4) were at least the
published map's, (b) each new class had a user's accuracy above its share of the held-out area, and (c) Crater, hills
and Normal Ground each lost under 5 points of producer's accuracy. %% KB §68.1--§68.4, §69.2, §69.8

\subsection{Against earlier automated mapping}

Each product here falls short of the method built for its own landform, and the comparison shows where. A
convolutional network on lunar elevation models recovers 92\% of a human-made test set's craters
\citep{Silburt2019}; fill depth on the Mars model recovers 52.4\% of the catalogued craters $\ge5$~km and does not see
degraded or breached rims, which a learned detector was not tried on here. The global valley map of \citet{Luo2009}
was found by an algorithm on topography and then edited by eye; unedited, the routed network here meets the valley
lines of \citet{Alemanno2018} no more often than the same map rotated in longitude, so the editing is the method, and
the candidate review stands in for it. DoMars16k \citep{Wilhelm2020} trains a network on 16,150 Context Camera samples
of fifteen landforms; the classes here rest on 971 polygons at 200~m, which is why every score sits beside a
geography-only baseline and why the U-Net's lead reads as a province map. Unsupervised classes from topographic
attributes \citep{Bue2006} need no labels but name nothing; the supervised maps here name their classes and are scored
on blocks they never saw. What the stack adds is the one grid: the three families are mapped from co-registered
visible, thermal and elevation data, and the thermal bands' share is measured rather than assumed, small (1.4 to 2.0
points on four classes) and, by balanced accuracy, absent.

and few valleys are mapped. The routed network is the elevation model's drainage, not a valley map.
